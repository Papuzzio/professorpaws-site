When homework stops before it starts · Ages 8–13

Homework help for kids who shut down, rush, or melt down over schoolwork.

Professor Paws takes the work school actually sent home and turns it into one calm step at a time — teaching the next step instead of handing over the answer.

No new homework. Just tonight's, made smaller.

You don't pick a curriculum. You add tonight's actual homework — type it or paste it in — and Professor Paws builds the practice around it.

  • Spelling

    This week's list, or your child's own words. The word is spoken, then written.

  • Maths

    Times tables, fractions, telling the time, carrying and borrowing — with things to move, not just numbers to stare at.

  • Reading

    Passages read aloud, with each word able to highlight as it's spoken, then questions about what actually happened.

  • Handwriting

    Letter-by-letter tracing that fades as the hand learns it. Strokes never leave the iPad.

Set it up once. Then you can leave the room.

You add the homework. After that it's your child's — nobody has to sit beside them reading the instructions out loud.

  1. Add tonight's homework

    Type or paste the assignment. Two lines is enough — "spelling list" and the ten words. (Photographing the worksheet is coming soon.)

  2. One step at a time

    Professor Paws breaks it down and teaches the step, with something to move on screen. If your child is stuck, the hint shows them how — it never fills in the answer.

  3. See where the help was needed

    Afterwards you get the honest version: what was easy, what took three tries, and which skill to look at again.

Built for the child who says "I can't do it" before they've read it.

Built for the child who could do it yesterday and can't today. Who reads the words but can't get started. Who rushes it just to make it stop. That shows up in the details, not in a badge.

  • When a full page is the problem

    One step on the screen at a time. Nothing to scan, nothing to lose your place in, no wall of questions.

  • When reading the question is the hard part

    Anything on the page can be read aloud, and a reading passage can highlight each word as it's spoken — so a child who decodes slowly can still follow the story.

  • When numbers on a page mean nothing yet

    Blocks, coins, bars and clock hands the child actually drags — the screen shows the problem and never the answer.

  • When being wrong is the thing they're avoiding

    No timer, no streak to break, and a wrong answer never costs them anything they've earned. It gets a smaller step, not a red cross.

You don't need a diagnosis, a referral, or a label. If you recognised your child in that list, this was built for them.

Where we actually are.

Professor Paws is in private beta. Rather than borrow credibility we haven't earned, here's the honest picture.

  • Private beta

    Real families are testing the iPad app now and telling us what's broken. Their feedback is why the app changes every week.

  • Teach, don't test

    The lesson design follows published education research on worked examples and fading support — teaching the step, then stepping back.

  • No outcome promises

    We won't tell you it raises grades. We haven't measured that, and anyone who claims it without measuring is guessing.

No reviews, ratings or family counts appear on this page because the beta is still small. When there are real ones, they'll go here — with permission, and no invented names.
